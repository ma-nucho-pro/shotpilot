# Photorealism and anti-AI reference

Use this only when the user wants a photograph, realistic render, or phone/camera look.

## Core rule

Reality is specific and imperfect. Improve realism through physical consistency, not generic quality adjectives.

### Lighting
Choose a real source: window daylight, direct flash, fluorescent ceiling fixtures, street lamps, overcast sky, golden-hour backlight, practical lamps. Define direction and shadow behavior.

### Camera
Choose a plausible capture style. A casual phone photo should not read like a perfect studio campaign. A studio product image can be cleaner but must still obey optical/material physics.

### Materials and skin
Use observable texture: pores, fabric weave, scuffs, fingerprints, condensation, metal patina, glass reflections, hair flyaways, stitching, paper fibers. Select only what belongs in the scene.

### Pose and expression
Prefer natural weight distribution, asymmetry, mid-action, believable gaze, and micro-expression over catalog poses when candid realism is requested.

### Imperfections
Add 2–4 context-appropriate imperfections, for example:
- slight exposure falloff or clipped highlight near a strong light source;
- subtle motion blur on moving hair/hand;
- minor clothing wrinkles;
- uneven skin sheen or visible pores;
- slightly imperfect background clutter/reflections;
- restrained sensor/phone noise or film grain.

Do not add all of them. Imperfections must support the capture style.

### Negative hygiene
Useful photographic negatives can include plastic skin, beauty-filter smoothing, malformed hands, duplicate limbs, floating objects, impossible reflections, oversaturated HDR, watermark, unwanted text, or logo artifacts. Only include negatives relevant to the request.
